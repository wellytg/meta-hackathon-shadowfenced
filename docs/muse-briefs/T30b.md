# T30b — Fewer false warnings in re-identification, cleaner generalised text

An independent set of cases (written without seeing the code) shows the detector catches risky combinations well
but warns on too many safe messages. The failure categories were:
- a single detail on its own (a date of birth with nothing else, a birth year, a lone first name) produced a warning;
- a public figure's widely published birthday in their public role;
- business contact details (a company's street address, phone and email, or a named business);
- aggregate statements about groups ("staff aged 30–40 at the clinic", "people born in 1990");
- a job title plus a town with no name or employer.

1. Change the scoring so that: one quasi-identifier alone never warns; a birth year alone or an age alone never
   counts as a date of birth; details attached to an organisation (company names with Ltd/Inc/LLC/Clinic/Pty,
   "our office at", "headquarters", "contact us", business email local parts like info@, sales@, support@) are not
   treated as a private person's details; aggregate or plural phrasing ("people", "staff aged", "customers born",
   percentages) does not count; a public role indicator ("the actor", "the senator", "the singer", "CEO of") lowers
   the weight of name + birthday. Keep these as documented rules in `reid.py` and `docs/REID.md`.
2. Avoid double counting the same span (a street name also detected as a person's name; a date counted as both a
   date of birth and a dated event).
3. Generalised text must read naturally: "born in 1991", "living in Fairhaven, WA", "postcode 982**", no repeated
   words ("the the"), and person names generalised to initials only when they are a person's name.
4. Add tests for each category above with new examples you write yourself (do not look for or use any other test
   set), keep all existing tests passing, and keep `scripts/eval.py --corpus reid` at its current catch rate.
   Run `python -m pytest -q`. Do not commit. Do not use the network.
